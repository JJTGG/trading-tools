01

Foundations

Core concepts

What is trading?

Trading is the buying and selling of financial instruments. A trade creates exposure to a price, and the result depends on what happens after entry.

Related: entry · exit · position · exposure
Market participants

Retail traders, institutions, brokers, exchanges, market makers, funds, and other participants interact through orders and transactions.

Related: liquidity · order flow · execution
Price discovery

Prices emerge through interaction between buying and selling interest. Bid, ask, spread, depth, and available liquidity influence that process.

Related: bid · ask · spread · liquidity
Trading plan

A trading plan describes the intended market, direction, entry, invalidation, target, position size, and conditions that change the thesis.

Related: setup · stop · target · risk
02

Markets

Where price forms

Spot trading

Spot trading involves buying or selling the underlying instrument rather than a derivative contract based on it.

Related: asset · settlement · exchange
Margin and derivatives

Derivatives can provide exposure to an underlying market without directly holding the asset. Margin and leverage change the capital required for that exposure.

Related: leverage · margin · liquidation
Forex

Forex markets quote one currency against another. Position value, spread, and price movement are interpreted through the currency pair.

Related: pair · pip · quote currency
Crypto markets

Cryptocurrency markets operate across multiple venues and can differ substantially in liquidity, volatility, market structure, and available data.

Related: token · pair · liquidity · volatility
Equity markets

Stocks represent ownership interests in companies. Trading conditions depend on the instrument, exchange, session, liquidity, and corporate events.

Related: share · exchange · session
03

Trading methods

Different approaches

Day trading

Positions are generally opened and closed within the same session, making execution, short-term volatility, and intraday risk important.

Related: intraday · session · volatility
Swing trading

Swing trading generally holds positions across multiple sessions to capture larger price movements than purely intraday trades.

Related: trend · timeframe · position
Position trading

Position trading generally uses longer holding periods and broader market structure, trend, or fundamental analysis.

Related: timeframe · trend · thesis
Arbitrage

Arbitrage strategies attempt to capture price differences between related markets or venues, after considering fees, latency, and execution.

Related: spread · fees · execution
Algorithmic trading

Algorithmic systems use explicit rules or models to analyze markets, produce signals, manage orders, or automate parts of a trading process.

Related: rules · signals · automation
Options

Options are contracts with defined rights and obligations. Strike, premium, expiration, volatility, and time affect their behavior.

Related: strike · premium · expiration
04

Risk management

Define the downside

Position size

Position size connects account risk to the distance between entry and invalidation. The same risk limit can produce very different sizes.

Open Position Size
Risk / reward

Risk / reward compares the defined downside distance with the defined target distance. It describes a setup rather than predicting its outcome.

Open Risk / Reward
Leverage

Leverage increases market exposure relative to available capital. That changes both capital efficiency and the consequences of adverse movement.

Open Leverage
PnL

PnL measures the financial result of a trade from entry, exit, direction, position size, and applicable fees.

Open PnL
Volatility

Volatility describes the magnitude of price movement. Greater volatility can increase the size of ordinary adverse movement.

Related: range · movement · risk
Liquidity and slippage

Lower liquidity can produce wider spreads, greater slippage, and more execution uncertainty, especially for larger orders.

Related: depth · spread · execution
05

Psychology

Decision behavior

Fear and greed

Emotional reactions can change entries, exits, position size, and willingness to follow a previously defined plan.

Related: discipline · decision
FOMO and chasing

Entering because a move has already happened can alter the original risk structure of a setup.

Related: entry · setup · risk
Confirmation bias

Traders may prefer information that supports an existing view. Explicit invalidation criteria can make the analysis less one-sided.

Related: thesis · invalidation
Revenge trading

Increasing risk to recover a previous loss changes the decision process and can compound an already negative outcome.

Related: discipline · sizing · risk
Review and journaling

Recording what was planned, what happened, and what changed provides material for reviewing decisions instead of only reviewing outcomes.

Related: record · review · workspace
06

Technical concepts

Market vocabulary

Price and volume

Price describes transaction levels while volume describes trading activity. Their meaning depends on the instrument and market structure.

Related: activity · liquidity
Support and resistance

Traders often use recurring areas of buying or selling interest as reference levels. These levels do not guarantee reversals.

Related: levels · structure
Trend and structure

Market structure can be described through sequences of highs and lows, ranges, breakouts, and changes in directional behavior.

Related: trend · range · breakout
Timeframes

The same instrument can behave very differently across timeframes. The chosen timeframe should match how the position is being planned and managed.

Related: session · swing · position
